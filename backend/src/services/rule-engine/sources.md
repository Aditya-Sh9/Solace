# Rule Engine — Source Citations

Every rule in this engine cites at least one peer-reviewed source or authoritative fact sheet.
These sources are NOT shown to users. They justify the rule thresholds and associations used.

## Deficiency Rules

### IRON — Iron deficiency fatigue
- NIH Office of Dietary Supplements. "Iron Fact Sheet for Health Professionals."
  https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-HealthProfessional/
  Relevant: fatigue and decreased energy are the most common symptoms of iron-deficiency anemia.
- Beard JL, Tobin B. "Iron status and exercise." Am J Clin Nutr. 2000.
  Relevant: inadequate iron-rich food intake correlates with reduced physical performance and fatigue.

### VITAMIN_D — Vitamin D + low mood + limited sunlight
- NIH Office of Dietary Supplements. "Vitamin D Fact Sheet for Health Professionals."
  https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/
  Relevant: skin exposure to UVB is the primary natural source of vitamin D; limited sunlight increases deficiency risk.
- Anglin RE, et al. "Vitamin D deficiency and depression in adults: systematic review and meta-analysis."
  Br J Psychiatry. 2013;202:100-107.
  Relevant: association between low vitamin D and depressive symptoms.

### MAGNESIUM — Magnesium + poor sleep + anxiety
- NIH Office of Dietary Supplements. "Magnesium Fact Sheet for Health Professionals."
  https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/
  Relevant: magnesium plays a role in supporting deep, restorative sleep and regulating the nervous system.
- Held K, et al. "Oral Mg(2+) supplementation reverses age-related neuroendocrine and sleep EEG changes."
  Pharmacopsychiatry. 2002;35(4):135-43.
  Relevant: magnesium involved in sleep regulation; deficiency associated with sleep disturbance and stress sensitivity.

### B12 — B12 + plant-based diet + cognitive fog
- NIH Office of Dietary Supplements. "Vitamin B12 Fact Sheet for Health Professionals."
  https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/
  Relevant: vitamin B12 is naturally found almost exclusively in animal foods; vegans and vegetarians are at higher risk of deficiency. Neurological symptoms include fatigue and cognitive difficulties.

### VITAMIN_C — Vitamin C + low energy + low fruit/veg intake
- NIH Office of Dietary Supplements. "Vitamin C Fact Sheet for Health Professionals."
  https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/
  Relevant: vitamin C is found primarily in fruits and vegetables; fatigue and malaise are early symptoms of inadequate intake.

## Lifestyle Rules

### SLEEP_DEBT — Chronic sleep restriction
- Grandner MA, et al. "Sleep duration, cardiovascular disease, and prediabetes: the NHANES." SLEEP. 2013.
  Relevant: average sleep below 6.5h/night associated with impaired daytime functioning and increased health risk.
- Pilcher JJ, Huffcutt AI. "Effects of sleep deprivation on performance: a meta-analysis."
  Sleep. 1996;19(4):318-26.
  Relevant: cumulative sleep debt (≥3 nights of <6h) produces measurable cognitive and mood impairment.

### DEHYDRATION — Chronic mild dehydration
- Popkin BM, D'Anci KE, Rosenberg IH. "Water, hydration, and health." Nutr Rev. 2010;68(8):439-58.
  Relevant: even mild dehydration (1–2% body weight) impairs mood, energy, and cognitive performance.
  General daily recommendation context: 8 glasses (2L) is the commonly cited benchmark; <5 glasses (1.25L) represents habitual under-hydration.

### SEDENTARY — Sedentary behaviour + limited sunlight
- Biswas A, et al. "Sedentary time and its association with risk for disease incidence." Ann Intern Med. 2015.
  Relevant: prolonged sedentary behaviour independent risk factor for metabolic and mood outcomes.
- NIH. "Sunlight, Vitamin D, and Health." Multiple ODS sources.
  Relevant: outdoor activity provides both physical movement and UVB exposure for vitamin D synthesis.

## Stress Rule

### SUSTAINED_STRESS — Chronic high stress
- McEwen BS. "Stressed or stressed out: What is the difference?" J Psychiatry Neurosci. 2005.
  Relevant: sustained perceived stress over 5+ days activates allostatic load mechanisms affecting sleep, mood, and immune function.
- Kivimäki M, et al. "Work stress and risk of cardiovascular mortality." BMJ. 2002.
  Relevant: chronic stress (≥5 consecutive high-stress days) is the threshold used in occupational health research.
